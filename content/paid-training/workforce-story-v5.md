# AI Mental OS v5 | What's in it for YOU?

**Status:** 30-slide, 150-minute first training draft. Opening replaced with the preferred collaboration animation; survey and live learner validation remain pending.

## The opening assertion

> **Learn AI to start useful work across disciplines sooner.** You do not become every specialist. You can get a good first pass for analysis, design, a draft or an operations checklist before another person's calendar is free. Bring better questions and more developed ideas to your colleagues. Human experts remain essential for consequential, frontier and regulated decisions.

**Primary motion:** [`motion/collaboration-with-ai.gif`](../../motion/collaboration-with-ai.gif). Authored renderer and avatar atlas remain in the repository. The deck embeds the exact committed GIF. It presents a hypothetical 2–3-day handoff delay as an **illustrative scenario, not measured average**. The animation holds its final comparison for about 11 seconds to let learners read.

**Older conceptual animation:** [`motion/human-value-knowledge-frontier.gif`](../../motion/human-value-knowledge-frontier.gif) remains useful optional context about specialization and changing work, but is not our main opening because it is less immediately relevant to the learner's daily friction.

## Opening arc, slides 1–7 (25 minutes)

1. Show the entire collaboration animation and ask what becomes possible with an immediate useful first pass (3 minutes).
2. Answer "What's in it for me?" with the **actual final animation frame**. Learners can explore, test and bring better prepared work to experts (3 minutes).
3. Explain the mechanism: fewer routine queues, not fewer people or fewer expert judgments (4 minutes).
4. Show four cross-functional starting points, with human specialist guardrails (4 minutes).
5. Ground workforce transformation in the ILO's task-exposure finding, not a job-loss forecast (4 minutes).
6. Acknowledge that productivity varies, including studies with slower outcomes. Checkability matters (4 minutes).
7. Participants identify one personal bottleneck and one meaningful human payoff (3 minutes).

## Paid-training connection

Once the audience wants that outcome, introduce **Mei**, whose AI-generated meeting notes invent approval. This makes the need for reliable delegation credible. The two teaching pillars remain **human above the loop** and **Outcome + Proof + Boundaries**. The rest of the 150-minute workshop shows how to specify, test, repair and stop a safe task before learners design their own 25th use case.

## The conclusion returns to the opening

> **You can start more work. You still own the result.**

No inflated personal time savings, no promise that experts become unnecessary, and no assertion that the LLM can reliably verify every claim by itself. Real checks and appropriate approvals remain part of the target result.
