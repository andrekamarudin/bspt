## Big picture first: 25 minutes before any 'tips'

1. **AI makes many first drafts easier, not automatically correct.** Distinguish generation from trusted outputs.
2. **Most roles face task change, not immediate total replacement.** ILO/NASK (20 May 2025): about one in four workers is in an occupation with some GenAI exposure; the ILO expects transformation to be more common than redundancy, because of continuing human input. Exposure is **not** a forecast of job losses. [ILO source](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-2025-update).
3. **The productivity effect varies by task.** Stanford HAI's 2026 Index summarizes a customer-support study with 14–15% more resolved issues per hour, but a separate study of experienced open-source developers reporting a 19% slowdown with AI help. These are separate samples and conditions, not an apples-to-apples effect estimate. [Stanford source](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy).
4. **Skills are shifting.** WEF's 2025 employer survey anticipated 39% of core skills would change or become outdated by 2030. This is an expectation, not an observed 2030 outcome. [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025).
5. **Human value shifts toward purpose and responsibility.** Our *interpretation*, not a scientific ranking: selecting useful problems, judging trade-offs, aligning people, and owning outcomes are important alongside technical expertise.
6. **Personal reflection:** Identify one safe task to delegate in order to spend more time on a meaningful human contribution.

## Story continues through one character

**Mei** is fictional. She asks for an AI meeting summary. The modeled output invents an approval and an owner not in the source notes. Rather than giving magic prompt tricks, we teach her to redefine the task:

- **Human above the loop:** Mei owns the purpose and permission boundaries. AI can draft, classify, check and repair permitted outputs, while external commitments or sends require exact approval.
- **Success provable by design:** Outcome + Proof + Boundaries. The output must classify source notes, cite the supporting line for each decision, mark unknowns and refrain from sending.

The learner sees a deliberately wrong example, a source-backed corrected register, and a **real, limited deterministic checker** with FAIL on the fabricated decision and PASS on the corrected known-answer fixture. The checker does not prove general semantic correctness; it verifies only the supplied case.

## Transfer to a new task

After guided practice, the learner must construct a new verifiable workflow not copied from Mei or the 24 free nuggets. The rubric checks: human outcome, specific deliverable, evidence source, testable criteria, bounded safe autonomy, and the right human approval/exception boundary.

**Quality bar:** Learner can explain the two principles and design a verifiable task unaided. Feelings of excitement and slide polish are not substitutes for demonstration.

## Story arc: six Ps, without six forced sections

Promise: learn how to direct AI work rather than babysit it. Problem: GenAI changes tasks but remains fallible. Pivot: above-loop oversight and outcomes designed for proof. Proof: source-backed examples, real checker and carefully sourced workforce research. Participation: reflection, guided contract exercise, independent capstone. Payoff: own the 25th safe AI workflow.
