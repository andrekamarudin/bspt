# Ask for the answer first, then the why

> **Tell a friend:** One answer, three reasons, one thing that could change it.

**Human problem:** A manager receives a long AI explanation and must hunt for the actual recommendation.

**Byte-size ProTip:** Demand the conclusion first, only decisive premises, one reversal condition and the next action.

## The before → after moment

**Safe fictional setup:** Question: 'Should our team switch every recurring report to a new dashboard tool?' No cost or adoption data supplied.

**Copy/paste demo prompt** (works in an ordinary text chat):

```text
Answer this for an attention-scarce manager: Should we switch every recurring report to a new dashboard tool? Start with ONE sentence. Then give the 2–4 premises that matter most, separate facts from assumptions, state what evidence would reverse your conclusion, and one next action. You have no actual cost/adoption data; do not invent them.
```

**What the learner should notice:** A sensible conditional recommendation emerges rather than invented certainty disguised as decisiveness.

## Now let them try

Paste a nonconfidential long explanation and ask for decision-first compression without losing material caveats.

**Evidence of learning:** The reader can repeat the conclusion, reasons and unresolved evidence in 30 seconds.

**Integrity guardrail:** Brevity cannot replace the evidence required for legal, safety or complex technical decisions.
