# Hire a polite critic who never gets tired

> **Tell a friend:** Before your boss challenges the idea, let the idea face a tougher question.

**Human problem:** Your team loves a plan and nobody wants to be the inconvenient person who asks what could go wrong.

**Byte-size ProTip:** Ask for the strongest fair objection and a low-cost test, not ten generic reasons to say no.

## The before → after moment

**Safe fictional setup:** Fictional idea: 'Let's replace all instructor-led onboarding with AI chat on Monday.'

**Copy/paste demo prompt** (no private data or paid integration required):

```text
Act as a constructive red-team partner, not a cheerleader. Proposal: 'Replace all instructor-led onboarding with AI chat on Monday.' Identify the THREE strongest failure modes, who would bear the cost, and one practical test for each. Then improve the proposal without discarding its potential upside. Separate measured facts from hypotheses.
```

**What the learner should notice:** Disagreement becomes a design tool, not a personal attack.

## Now let them try

Use a harmless idea you like; write down which objection actually changes your plan.

**Evidence of learning:** At least one objection is substantive, falsifiable and paired with a mitigation or test.

**Integrity guardrail:** AI objections are hypotheses, not expert findings; don't mistake generated skepticism for validated risk.
