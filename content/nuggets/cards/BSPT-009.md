# One idea. Three people. Three versions.

> **Tell a friend:** Same truth, different doorway.

**Human problem:** You describe a project accurately to a technician, but a finance leader and frontline colleague hear different priorities.

**Byte-size ProTip:** Keep claims constant while rewriting the opening, evidence and next step for different human stakes.

## The before → after moment

**Safe fictional setup:** Fact set: 'A new report refreshes daily instead of weekly. Accuracy and cost have not yet been evaluated.'

**Copy/paste demo prompt** (works in an ordinary text chat):

```text
Explain this same fact to (A) frontline staff, (B) finance manager, and (C) technical maintainer. Facts: a new report refreshes daily instead of weekly; accuracy and cost are not evaluated yet. Give each version a different human-relevant opening and next question, maximum 45 words each. Preserve the same facts and uncertainty. Then show what changed between versions.
```

**What the learner should notice:** Audience-centered communication differs from changing the truth.

## Now let them try

Have two learners compare which version speaks to them and whether any version exaggerates benefits.

**Evidence of learning:** No new facts appear; each audience sees why it should care.

**Integrity guardrail:** Never personalize by stereotyping job roles or asserting unmeasured ROI.
