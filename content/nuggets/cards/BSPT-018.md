# Three options beat a fake yes-or-no

> **Tell a friend:** The third option is often the one you forgot to consider.

**Human problem:** A team frames a change as 'buy the expensive tool or do nothing', ignoring a small trial.

**Byte-size ProTip:** Ask for distinct options, explicit criteria, reversibility and a recommendation conditioned on missing evidence.

## The before → after moment

**Safe fictional setup:** Fictional task: automate weekly reporting; options not yet considered beyond 'full rollout' and 'status quo'.

**Copy/paste demo prompt** (no private data or paid integration required):

```text
A fictional team wants to reduce manual weekly reporting. They are debating only 'full automation now' versus 'do nothing'. Create exactly THREE genuinely distinct options, including a reversible pilot. Compare expected benefits, failure modes, setup effort and what evidence each needs. Give a conditional recommendation. Do not invent monetary savings.
```

**What the learner should notice:** Binary frustration becomes a reversible experiment.

## Now let them try

Write your own stuck either/or and force the model to produce one cheaper reversible alternative.

**Evidence of learning:** Options differ materially and no unsupported numeric ROI is invented.

**Integrity guardrail:** A third option is not automatically better; compare against the actual objective and constraints.
