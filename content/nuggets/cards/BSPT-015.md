# Ask what would change your mind

> **Tell a friend:** A strong recommendation knows how it could be wrong.

**Human problem:** Two colleagues debate a software purchase for weeks because neither names the evidence that would settle it.

**Byte-size ProTip:** Define an explicit reversal condition and a discriminating experiment before arguing about preference.

## The before → after moment

**Safe fictional setup:** Fictional claim: 'Vendor A is best for us.' Unknowns: actual recurring cost, staff adoption, and recovery support.

**Copy/paste demo prompt** (no private data or paid integration required):

```text
Our fictional team favors Vendor A, but we don't know its real recurring cost, adoption rate or recovery support. Give a conditional recommendation, the 3 pieces of evidence most likely to reverse it, and the cheapest safe experiment to obtain one of them. If the available information cannot establish a winner, say so clearly.
```

**What the learner should notice:** The argument turns into a short learning plan.

## Now let them try

Choose a low-stakes contested decision and have each side write one reversal criterion.

**Evidence of learning:** At least one observable result would actually change the recommendation, not merely confirm it.

**Integrity guardrail:** Don't reduce ethical or non-negotiable constraints to a cost/benefit trade-off.
