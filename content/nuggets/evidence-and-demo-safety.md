# Evidence, claims and demonstration safeguards

This is a citation registry for the **general teaching principles**, not proof that these particular prompts will always work or that attendees want them. Check the source again before public instruction.

## Credible external grounding

1. [OpenAI, Prompt engineering best practices](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10032626) and [OpenAI, creating a good prompt](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936848-how-do-i-create-a-good-prompt-for-an-ai-model): be specific about the task and context; iterate rather than searching for magic words. Underlies 002, 007, 009 and 022.
2. [OpenAI, Does ChatGPT tell the truth?](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313428): AI responses can be confidently wrong, including invented citations and quotes. Important claims need external verification. Underlies 016 and every card's proof check.
3. [NIST AI RMF, Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework): evaluation, risk management, information integrity, privacy and human oversight matter beyond the output's fluency. Underlies 011, 012, 016, 019, 021 and 023.
4. [UNESCO, AI Competency Framework for Students](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students): human-centered AI, ethics, application skills and creative capability. Supports the learning goals and agency-centered design, not any individual model result.
5. [OpenAI, Prompting Academy resource](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/work-users-ynjqu/resources/prompting): iterative prompting, clarification and task-focused conversations. Underlies the first six learn cards.

## Model-neutral demo policy

- Use a **fresh standard text chat** whenever possible. Don't require search, uploads, accounts, workplace access, voice, code execution, paid features, or previously saved personal memory.
- Each synthetic snippet is **fictional**. For any input that looks sensitive, explicitly say it is invented and safe to paste into a training environment.
- Run each selected prompt with the **actual** model/client before a live event. Inspect output, don't present a predicted output as if recorded.
- Precalculate objective checks manually: 017 should show 8/10 = 80% vs 8/20 = 40%; 019 signed total -20.00 with all 3 rows; 020 bonus outputs 0,0,5,0; 021 toy cost 2 × 0.5h × 20 = 20.
- Have a no-internet fallback: read the fictional inputs and discuss what a strong result **should** contain. Do not fabricate a 'live AI' answer.
- Evaluate demonstration **behavior**, not tools, hype or speed-up estimates. The same prompt may yield different wording across runs.
- Never copy actual inboxes, employee records, policy documents, payroll, client details or private prompts into public chat systems without explicit organizational approval and appropriate controls.

## Claim language

**Safe:** “Try this approach on a low-risk task and check the output.” / “In this fictional example the total is -20.00.”

**Not safe without evidence:** “Everyone saves 30% of their time.” / “This model never makes mistakes.” / “Our audience ranked this the best.” / “One inspiring case proves the tool works for all teams.”

## Ethical story and facilitation

Human stakes may be confusion, hope, stress, dignity, confidence, belonging, family time or autonomy. Avoid fear-based job-loss messaging as a default. Never use a personal story as statistical proof, or invent a customer case to sell a capability. Ask participants to **own the outcome and the review**, not abdicate judgment to AI.
